# HEXFIELD 325 — One living artwork, not a sequence of resets

Build 325 gives the automatic painter a **durable visual identity** that can survive its procedural mutations, changing donors, local reinterpretations and composition negotiations.

## Actual lineage

After the **first accepted painting**, Hexfield derives a compact identity exclusively from the pixels of that accepted image: a root identifier, nine region-specific chromatic signatures, up to two physically recognized forms with normalized geometry and colour, and the past accepted relationships between regions. Only this metadata is saved locally; no persistent bitmaps or uploaded images are created.

Every new candidate inherits a little of that prior accepted visual identity **before** local KEEP/REWORK, cross-region voting and global W / φ / H assessment:

1. Each surviving shape carries **authentic pixels from the last accepted parent image** inside a bounded gently changing organic contour. The form can migrate slightly over generations, but cannot teleport or suddenly become an unrelated object. These bounded copies are small enough not to dominate or freeze the next artwork.
2. Nine region-specific measured palette constraints act slowly: a single neighbourhood can gently drift toward its existing chromatic ancestry, without imposing a flat global recolour or forcing all neighbours to be identical. Each channel's correction is capped; high-chroma detail is protected.
3. Successful historical regional relationships subtly influence which neighbouring areas are invited to discuss colour/geometry next, but **do not override the neighbours' actual votes**. The older independent region and composition memories continue to work.
4. All retained physical form locations are treated as protected ancestry during the subsequent regional rework and cross-neighbour debate.
5. The proposed child, including inherited pixels, revised regions, negotiations and final words, is **scored for real**. Only the globally selected finished candidate updates the root's form geometry, palette targets and relationship history. Losing trials cannot teach it preferences or overwrite its roots.

The root identifier persists across generations and browser reloads. A deliberate Reset starts a truly new visual identity. Different generations may retain recognisable visual tendencies without looking like duplicate slides.

## Performance and safeguards

The identity is based on one 27×18 sampling readback, up to two actual source-image form copies and at most one local pigment adjustment. No new controls, cloud calls or heavyweight model inference.

User-locked art laws and already verified sparse local repainting take precedence over visual-identity adaptation; stored identity is inert for those operations. Text is composed afterward as part of the actual evaluated canvas. Paused or hidden browsing does not prematurely commit identity. Local metadata is strictly validated and bounded before loading; no full image is stored in localStorage.

This is a **procedural visual personality**, not semantic self-awareness or human-level aesthetic judgement. The form identifiers represent clustered colour/shape components actually extracted from painted pixels; they aren't named physical objects.

## Validation

Build 325 adds tests for actual nine-region palette measurement, stable root IDs, gradual inheritance, the same form carrying over while moving only slightly, genuine last-accepted-pixel copies, safe regions, small data storage, user locks, sparse rendering, historical visual relationships and ten generations retaining one visual lineage. Test passing establishes mechanical behaviour, not real-device artistic quality.
